How a programme looks

Five questions about how a programme shows up in the app (spec §10). The data is the same everywhere: Beginner 5×5, eight weeks from Mon 23 Nov; today is Wed 16 Dec, in week 4, a deload week. Deadlift is paused for two weeks (knee), Barbell row has a hand-edit in week 6, and Bench press was lowered on Wed 9 Dec, which split week 3.

What stays as it is: the week view outside its header, a paused row and a locked day; an item page's other rows; the Dialog, the buttons, the chips and the marks. Every screen is live: press, scroll and unfold. Try it full size, on your phone too.

1. The overview at phone width

The programme’s own page: every activity against every week. Eight weeks don’t fit across a 390px phone. Which shape does the plan take there, and on a desktop? The cells use question 2’s option A here.

A. A table that scrolls sideways

Option A

One column per week, the activity column fixed at the left. On a phone two and a half weeks show, and the table opens on this week. Every week reads the same way, at the cost of a sideways swipe. Try A full size

2. Locked, hand-edited and paused cells

The same in every option: a past week shows how it went (“3 of 3”, in pen and underlined when the goal was met, as Recent weeks does) over what was planned; this week shows what to lift and how many so far; a later week shows what to lift; a deload week says “Deload” under its dates; a split week reads “35 → 32.5 kg from Wed”. On a tile a met count is ink with its pen underline, because pen text measures 4.49:1 on the light tile, just under 4.5:1. What differs is how a locked week, a hand-edited value and a paused activity look. Press Barbell row’s later weeks to open the change sheet.

A. Icons

Option A

A lock beside each past week’s name, once per week rather than in every cell; a pen after a value set by hand (the app’s pencil already means “changed by you”); a pause sign with “Paused” and the reason. This week on a tile. Recommended: the smallest marks, the same in the table and the timeline, and each one named for a screen reader (“set by hand on 12 Dec”).

3. The change sheet and its preview

Every change shows what it does before it happens (spec P2). The example: Barbell row set to 45 kg from Mon 21 Dec, from then on. Week 6 has a hand-edit (35 kg, set on 12 Dec), which a later change keeps unless you drop it. Press “Drop it too”, or switch to “That week only”, and the preview follows. The sheet is the Dialog card, nothing new; on a phone it keeps 16px round it.

A. One sheet, the preview under the fields

Option A

What to change, from when, for how long and why, then “What changes”, then Confirm. Recommended: the preview follows every choice as you make it, and Confirm sits straight after what it confirms. On a phone you scroll to it. Try A full size

4. The activity page’s Programme row

An item in a programme gets a Programme row on its page, first, where “This week’s plan” would be: where it has been, where it goes, and its controls (Set weight, Set increase, Pause, End). Back squat shows a change of rate on Thu 3 Dec; Deadlift shows a pause. The Notes row under it is the ItemPage card’s, unchanged.

A. A step chart

Option A

The planned weight a step a week, solid to this week and dashed after it; a dot on each week done, filled in pen where the goal was met; a gap where it paused; a dashed line and a label where a change took effect. Hover a week for its numbers; a screen reader gets a table. Recommended: the shape of the plan (deloads, the steeper rate, the pause) reads at a glance, and the numbers are a press away. 5. The week view’s programme header

A programme tracker on the week: its header says which programme week this is and leads to the overview; Monday is locked (marks stay open for one day, so Tuesday can still be marked); Deadlift is paused. Monday’s name is green and underlined because two were marked, as any day that meets the daily pick. The rest is the week as PlannerScreen draws it.

A. “Week 4 of 8” as a link on the title’s line

Option A

The link sits where Edit plan puts Add, at the end of the title’s line. A lock beside a locked day’s name; its boxes stay as they are, they just don’t press. A paused row says so in place of its boxes: “Paused until Mon 28 Dec. Knee.” Recommended: one line of header, and a paused row that can’t be mistaken for a week off. Try A full size
