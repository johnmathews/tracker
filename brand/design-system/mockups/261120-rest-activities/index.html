Rest these activities

An exception week can already rest one activity: Add values for one activity, Add a value, Times a week, Set to 0. Resting the legs takes that three times over. This mockup adds a list to the exception week: tick the activities that rest in its weeks. A rest sets the activity’s Times a week to 0 for those weeks. The week after carries on as planned, as after a deload. Everything else in the exception week stays as it is.

Each option is drawn on a phone first, 390 wide at real size, then on a laptop at 1280 × 800, scaled to fit. Scroll inside a stage to see the rest of the page. The programme is Beginner 5×5. Week 3 rests the legs: Back squat and Deadlift. Week 6 rests the chest and arms: Bench press and Barbell row.

Open question

1. How you pick the activities that rest

The list sits under Label, before the values. Its first entry is Every activity, for a week away. The ticked activities show in the closed exception week as “Rest: Back squat, Deadlift.” The grid says “Rest week” in their cells for those weeks, as it already does for a planned week of 0. Values for other activities still work as today.

A. A checklist

Option A

One row per activity, each with a checkbox, under “Rest these activities”. Each row is 48 px tall and the whole row is the target. This adds a component to the system, Checkbox: a 24 px box with a 2 px control-border, filled ink with an on-ink tick. No colour is new. Recommended: the Switch card says a form with a Save uses a checkbox, and a list reads top to bottom like the Activities section above it.

B. Pressed buttons

Option B

One button per activity, side by side, wrapping onto new lines. A pressed button is filled ink, as a SegmentedControl’s choice is, with a tick before its name. It takes less height, and six activities fit in two or three lines on a phone. Unlike a SegmentedControl, several can be pressed at once, which the look doesn’t say.
