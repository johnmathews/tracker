The planner's layout: the week fixed at the top, Exercises, Cardio and Food each filling the screen under it, the page sprinting from one to the next, and the Header out of sight until it is wanted.

## Use

- **The week, fixed at the top** (K88). The page's `<h1>`, "19–25 October", is a band at the top centre that stays put while the screens move under it: `title-1` (the serif, 600) at `--fs-title` (max(30px, 40u); 30n folded), in `ink`, on `canvas`, 28u above it and 12u below (34n and 8px folded). Its height is `--week-band`. At the top of Exercises it has no edge; once a screen is under it, a 1px hairline in `line` shows at its foot. The touch handle (Header) lies over its middle.
- **One section per screen** (K24). Exercises is the first screen, Cardio the second and Food the third (K90). Each is at least the window's height less the band (`100dvh - --week-band`). The page's `scroll-padding-top` is the band's height, so focus never lands under it.
- **Sprinting with a mouse, snapping on touch** (K38, K98). A screen with more rows than fit scrolls as usual. The page never comes to rest showing part of two screens: once the line between them is a step (4% of the window, at least 16px) inside it, the page sprints on in the direction it was going, down so the next screen's top meets the band's foot, or up so the earlier screen's end meets the bottom of the window (K45, K88). A wheel, or a fling's momentum after the finger lifts, sprints while it is still moving; a finger on the screen holds the page; a scrollbar drag or the keyboard once the scroll stops (120ms still). The sprint takes 260ms, eased out, at once when motion is reduced, and a push moves one screen (K94, K95): its momentum, however long it runs, is ignored, but every new push counts, so two swipes move two screens. A new push is a new gesture after the wheel was still for 150ms, a mouse wheel's click after 100ms of silence, or a new swipe inside the momentum, whose speed drops and then grows again. Nothing moves on while a sprint runs (K96): a small scroll moves one screen, never two. A wheel the other way stops the sprint where it is and goes straight back (K89). **The screens wrap round** (K92): a new wheel gesture or a swipe that starts at the page's foot and goes a step on brings the first screen up from below, and one at the top going up brings the last down from above, in the same 260ms sprint. A copy of the screen, inert and hidden from a screen reader, stands in beyond the end while it moves, then the page jumps to the real one, which looks the same. A fling that reaches the foot of a tall screen stops there. A resize or a rotation that leaves the page across the line settles on the screen that fills more of the window (K40). With a mouse or trackpad this is script, not CSS snapping: with `scroll-snap-type: y mandatory`, a mouse wheel at the end of a tall screen snapped back instead of crossing to the next. **On a touch screen the browser snaps** (K98): `scroll-snap-type: y mandatory` with `scroll-snap-stop: always` on each screen, and `scroll-padding-top` the band's height, so a swipe, however hard, stops at the next screen under the week and its momentum is the browser's own. The screen shown, when taller than the window, has stops inside it (K101, K102), every 80% of the window and at its foot, so each swipe shows the next part of it before the next screen comes: iOS won't scroll freely inside a snap area taller than the window. Only the screen shown has them, so a swipe up onto another screen lands on its top. The script only runs the arrows there, with snapping off while they move. The wrap round is a snap too (K100): a copy of the last screen stands above the first and of the first below the last, so a swipe past an end snaps onto it like any screen, and once the page rests it jumps to the real screen, which looks the same. A script that set the position each frame fought iOS's momentum: it overshot, and holding the page against it jittered.
- **Inside a screen:** a column holding the SectionHeader, then its WeekGrid, which takes the rest of the height (WeekGrid, Filling the screen). When the week is shorter than the screen it sits in the middle. Padding follows the screen's scale `u` (WeekGrid): 12u under the band, 48u at the sides, 20u at the foot. Folded (held upright), 10px under the band and 16n at the sides.
- **The arrows between the screens** (K42, K90): a quiet IconButton with a 24px Lucide `chevron-down`, no words, centred at the foot of each screen but the last, in `ink-secondary`; and one with `chevron-up` on each screen but the first, centred on its title's line just under the band. A down arrow sprints to the next screen's top and an up arrow to the one before (the sprint above). So Exercises has one down, Cardio one up and one down, and Food one up. A short screen leaves them out.
- **The bar** is the Header, out of sight (Header, Out of sight): a long press anywhere, the handle or E opens Edit plan, where the bar is sticky above the page (K80, K91); focus floats it over the screen for the keyboard.
- **Messages** (Banner: sync, confirm your email, import) sit at the top of the Exercises screen, under the band and above its SectionHeader, and push the section down while they show.
- **Every screen size gets this layout.** A desktop, an iPad on its side and a phone on its side get the wide grid; a screen held upright under 1000px wide, or any screen under 760px wide, gets the folded one (K26). There is no separate phone view.
- **Edit plan is one tracker per screen too** (K99). Each section is at least the window's height less the sticky bar's (`--bar-height`, which the Header measures), starts at the top with Edit plan's own rows (max(76px, 100u), K54), and leaves any space over empty at its foot. The page moves between them exactly as above, each screen resting just under the bar instead of the week. The arrows show: the way back on its own line above the title, the way on centred on the Add button's line under the grid. The week's `<h1>` is hidden on screen there, since Edit plan's bar shows the week (K81), and shows in `display` only on paper.
- **Not in Settings**: there the page scrolls freely, nothing sprints and there is no bar (K52).
- **Print**: no sprinting, no bar, the WeekGrid as a table on paper. Each section starts a page of its own, with the week over it in `display` (SectionHeader, K97).

## States

| State | Look |
| --- | --- |
| At rest | the week fixed at the top (K86, K88), then the screen: the section's heading, the week's grid, and the arrows to the screens before and after it |
| Moving, or on Cardio or Food | a screen under the band, with the hairline at the band's foot |
| Bar called up | the Header over the top of the screen, with `shadow-overlay` |
| Short (540px tall or less) | no band (the week is for a screen reader only); the title in the grid's corner; no chips, no arrows |
| Folded (upright under 1000px wide, or under 760px wide) | the WeekGrid folded; the band at 30n |

## Accessibility

- Each screen is a `<section aria-labelledby>` on its SectionHeader's `<h2>`. The band is the week's `<h1>`, before them all, so the heading outline is unchanged; where the band is hidden it stays for a screen reader.
- The sprint never traps the keyboard: focus moving into the Cardio or Food grid scrolls it into view, and a stop across a line sprints on to the next screen.
- Each arrow is a real `<button>` named for where it goes: "Go to Cardio", "Back to Exercises", "Go to Food" and "Back to Cardio".
- Nothing depends on hover: a long press, the handle and focus reach the bar with a mouse, a finger or the keyboard. Only focus lays it over the week.
